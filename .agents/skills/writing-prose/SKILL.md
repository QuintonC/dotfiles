# Writing prose

Apply these rules to the artifact. Use the global communication policy for the surrounding response.

## Precedence

1. Factual and safety requirements
2. The user's requested format, tone, or style
3. An artifact-specific skill or template
4. The artifact's audience and established author voice
5. These writing defaults

Use a practical adaptation of ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE) when it is compatible with higher-priority requirements. Do not force STE when it would damage the requested voice, audience fit, or required format. Do not claim formal ASD-STE100 compliance.

## Terms and mechanics

1. Write **ecommerce**, never e-commerce, Ecommerce, or eCommerce. Write **internet** in lowercase.
2. Use American English.
3. Use the Oxford comma.
4. Use sentence case for titles.
5. Use one space between sentences.

## Clear language

- Do not use idioms or insider language.
- Explain an acronym at its first use. Remove acronyms that do not help the reader.
- Add a link when it gives useful context. Do not repeat the linked content without a reason.
- Avoid unnecessary references to your team, company, or product. Use one reference at most when it gives necessary context.
- Use the same term for the same concept.

## Voice and layout

- Prefer active voice. Use passive voice when the concrete object is the useful subject.
- Prefer positive statements when they preserve the meaning.
- Use short sentences for key information. Vary sentence length when the audience and voice benefit from it.
- Put a blank line between paragraphs. Do not indent paragraphs.
- Use bold and italic text only when the emphasis helps the reader.

## Polish

- Match the editing effort to the audience and formality.
- Prefer the shortest complete draft.
- Use a natural tone unless the artifact is legal, regulatory, or requires formal language.
- Preserve the established author voice unless the user requests a change.

## Two revision methods

1. Move the second-to-last paragraph to the start. Then remove the text that is no longer necessary.
2. Ask: "Would I explain this to a colleague in the same plain language?" If not, use the plain-language explanation.

## Self-check

1. **Content value:** Does each sentence add information? Remove text that repeats the diff, a link, or nearby context.
2. **Names:** Is the team, company, or product named more than once? Remove unnecessary names.
3. **Voice:** Can active voice or a positive statement make the meaning clearer without changing it?
4. **Terminology:** Explain necessary acronyms. Replace idioms, insider language, and inconsistent terms.
5. **Clarity:** Is this the shortest complete draft in language that fits the audience and author voice?
